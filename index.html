How to read this chapter: Hunt is mostly on Marx's side here. He explains Marx, defends him from common misreadings, and criticizes him in only a couple of spots (marked "Hunt's view").
1 · The critique

Capitalism is historical

Classical economists lacked historical perspective. Slave society, feudalism, and capitalism are different modes of production. Two confusions followed:

  • Capital is universal. Tools exist everywhere; they become capital only when they bring profit to an owning class. (Hodgskin was the exception.)
  • Economics is exchange. Bastiat: "political economy is exchange." Looking only at the market, everyone seems free, equal, and in harmony.

Against Mill: the form of property decides distribution, so production and distribution aren't separate.

2 · Commodities

Use value & value

  • Use value: usefulness. Different qualities, not comparable.
  • Exchange value: trading ratio, shown as price.
  • Value: the common substance, socially necessary labor time. Skilled labor counts as a multiple of simple labor.
  • Useful labor makes use values; abstract labor makes value.
  • Labor done privately becomes social only when the product sells. People's relations appear as relations between things.
3 · The circuits

C–M–C vs. M–C–M′

  • C–M–C sell to buy; ends with a use value.
  • M–C–M′ buy to sell dearer; never ends. M′ − M = surplus value.
  • Exchange can't create it: equal trades gain nothing, unequal trades just move value around.
  • Industrial capital: M–C…P…C′–M′. Merchant and interest-bearing capital are "derivative": they share surplus made elsewhere.
4 · The secret of profit

Labor vs. labor power

  • Workers sell labor power (capacity to work), worth the subsistence of their family. Subsistence is cultural, not biological.
  • The labor they actually perform creates more value than that.
  • Workers are "free in a double sense": legally free, and free of any means of production.
  • Capitalism = a small class owns the means of production; everyone else must sell labor power or starve.
5 · The formulas

c, v, s

  • Constant capital c: machines, materials. Only passes on its own value.
  • Variable capital v: labor power. Its value grows.
  • Value = c + v + s
  • Rate of surplus value s/v = surplus ÷ necessary labor = rate of exploitation.
  • Organic composition c/v
  • Rate of profit r = s/(c+v) = (s/v)/(c/v+1)
  • Capitalists fight to lengthen the working day. "Accumulate, accumulate!"
6 · Values and prices

The transformation problem

  • Value is the essence; price is its appearance.
  • Competition equalizes s/v and r, but c/v differs by industry. So prices of production must differ from values.
  • High c/v → price above value. Low c/v → price below value. Surplus value is redistributed, not lost.
  • Flaw: Marx transformed outputs but not inputs. Full solutions (Bortkiewicz on) keep only two of three equalities.
  • Hunt's view: charging Marx with a logical error is wrong.
7 · Origins

Primitive accumulation

The economists' story: a frugal elite saved, the lazy spent. Marx compared it to original sin. The real history was force:

  • Peasants driven off land (enclosures, church and common lands seized), then whipped into wage discipline.
  • Fortunes from American gold and silver, slavery, colonial looting, national debt, protectionism, all using state power.
  • "Force is the midwife of every old society pregnant with a new one."
8 · Laws of motion

Where accumulation leads

  • Concentration: the strong absorb the weak; technology raises the minimum capital.
  • Falling rate of profit: c/v rises. Offsets: speed-ups, wages below value, cheaper constant capital, foreign trade. A tendency, not a forecast; total profit can still rise.
  • Crises: reserve army → labor-saving machines → demand falls short → imbalance between capital-goods and consumer-goods sectors → general glut. About a 10-year cycle.
  • Alienation and increasing misery, even if wages rise.
9 · Hunt's verdict

What holds up

  • Weak spot: Marx didn't connect c/v and s/v. New technology can raise real wages and s/v; "capital-saving" innovation may keep c/v from rising.
  • Marx didn't foresee repression turning misery into apathy and despair instead of revolution.
  • The Soviet collapse doesn't disprove him: he analyzed capitalism, and only capitalism can prove him wrong.
